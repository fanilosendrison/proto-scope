# DerivTrace falsification corpora

The following corpora are candidates for bounded experiments. Their inclusion
does not validate a conceptual model, establish coverage, or grant normative
authority to an observation or reconstruction.

## Corpus A — STOP of coding agents

This corpus is intended to examine execution-contract defects, missing
premises, contradictory constraints, forgotten dependencies, insufficient
evidence, and subsequent corrections.

A future corpus selection must define its boundaries and provenance. It must not
assume that an agent's unexposed internal reasoning can be observed.

## Corpus B — Turnlock

This corpus is intended to examine relationships among Product Intent,
invariants, claims, justifications, and coverage.

A historical reference is available in
[Turnlock experiments](../../specification-methodology/2026-10-15/07-experiences-turnlock.md),
including TL-INV-008, TL-CLAIM-010, and TL-CLAIM-011. That material remains a
historical, non-normative research record and is not modified by this work.

## Evidence distinctions

Every future corpus entry or result must distinguish among:

- **historical observation:** evidence recorded at the time by the cited source;
- **analytical reconstruction:** a later account reconstructed from available
  evidence;
- **research hypothesis:** a proposition retained for examination rather than
  accepted as true;
- **verified conclusion:** a conclusion supported only within the explicit
  inputs, method, scope, assumptions, and evidence of its verification.

None of these categories acquires normative authority merely by being recorded
in DerivTrace.

## Public-data boundary

No private report, secret, credential, sensitive identifier, private
infrastructure detail, or non-public content may be copied into this public
repository. Future experiments must use public material or a safely redacted,
provenance-preserving representation that does not disclose protected content.

Existing historical corpora remain unchanged.
